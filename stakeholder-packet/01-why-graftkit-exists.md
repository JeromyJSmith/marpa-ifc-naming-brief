# Why Graftkit Exists

MARPA's work is not just drawings, estimates, plants, or renderings. It is a connected design and construction system. A single project can contain Vectorworks files, sheets, layers, classes, plant records, schedules, PDFs, worksheets, IFC exports, images, vendor information, historical pricing, and client-facing presentation material.

The problem is that those pieces usually live in different places and do not naturally explain each other.

Graftkit exists to connect them.

## The Business Reason

The firm needs a system that can answer practical questions with evidence:

- What exactly was designed?
- Where did this quantity come from?
- Which drawing, layer, class, record, or worksheet supports it?
- What does this object become in IFC or BIM review?
- What plant, material, or hardscape asset does it represent?
- What cost code or estimating group does it belong to?
- How does it affect budget, scope, and schedule?
- Can the answer be shown to a stakeholder in a way that makes sense?

Graftkit is the governed foundation for answering those questions.

## The Product Idea

Graftkit is a project intelligence system for landscape architecture, design-build operations, and AEC data work.

It combines:

- Vectorworks extraction and writeback planning;
- IFC/BIM/GIS structure;
- Pixeltable evidence and enrichment;
- DuckDB/DuckLake analytical snapshots;
- Marimo review and modeling surfaces;
- plant and hardscape asset management;
- financial intelligence and predictive estimating;
- governed agent workflows with receipts.

## What Makes This Different

This is not a generic project management app. It is not a simple website. It is not a one-off notebook.

Graftkit is built around source evidence. Every important output should point back to the file, object, table, run, or receipt that produced it.

That matters because design and construction data is messy. If the system cannot say where a number came from, it is not ready to drive estimating, client review, BIM validation, or writeback into Vectorworks.

## What The Stakeholder Should Expect

Stakeholders should expect a system that makes MARPA's process more legible:

- project evidence becomes visible;
- design objects become traceable;
- costs become explainable;
- renderings and technical BIM surfaces stop competing;
- plant and asset data gets a proper home;
- agent work becomes auditable instead of invisible.

The result is a stronger operational foundation for design, estimating, review, and future automation.
