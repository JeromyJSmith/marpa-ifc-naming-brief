Geo-referenced platform

Georeferencing is the foundation for spatial design intelligence

A project needs a verified coordinate reference system, True North, parcel identity, elevation reference, and IFC map conversion before cost, shadow, wildfire, watershed, and regulatory checks can be trusted.

Ground truth stack

The coordinate stack has multiple anchors, not one point on a map

WGS84 latitude / longitude GPS, web maps, GeoJSON, and IFC site reference.
NAD83 / UTM Zone 13N Denver-Boulder survey and GIS work; useful statewide reference for many Colorado datasets.
Colorado State Plane North, Central, and South zones for county GIS, survey, and legal description alignment.
Cartesian project origin The Vectorworks/BIM internal origin tied back to the real-world coordinate reference.
NAVD88 elevation Orthometric height for grading, drainage, floodplain, and watershed analysis.
North matters

Shadow studies require True North, not an assumed drawing direction

Seasonal shadow studies

With true location, date, time, and north rotation, Vectorworks can simulate sunlight and shadows across seasons. That lets MARPA see shade, heat, comfort, plant performance, view impacts, and solar exposure while the design is still flexible.

True North vs. Magnetic North vs. Grid North

True North supports solar studies and surveys. Magnetic North drifts. Grid North is a mapping convenience. Treating them as interchangeable corrupts sun angle, shadow direction, and downstream environmental analysis.

3D planting decisions

Once trees and plants become 3D objects, MARPA can evaluate mature canopy, shadows, spatial conflicts, species risk, maintenance context, and cost impact from the same object model.

Legal parcel identity

The project also needs to know which legal property it belongs to

APN / assessor identity

Links the project to tax records, zoning, permit history, county GIS, and jurisdiction-specific review context.

PLSS and recorded plat

Township, range, section, quarter-section, plat book, and recorded references anchor the design to legal land description.

Address and place identity

USPS/CASS, county address, and Google Place ID help reconcile the postal address with the legal and mapped parcel.

Control points

Survey monuments or four-point control keep the model from drifting against parcel boundaries, imagery, and site measurements.

Colorado wildfire context

Location turns code changes into spatial design checks

Colorado's wildfire-resiliency work is spatial: WUI area, fire intensity, defensible space, plant rules, and material requirements depend on where the project is and what jurisdiction controls the parcel.

Find the jurisdiction and wildfire area

Latitude, longitude, parcel, address, and project boundary determine whether the site sits inside a mapped wildfire area and which local code language applies.

↓

Create structure ignition zones

Generate 0-5 ft, 5-30 ft, and 30-100 ft zones around buildings, decks, stairs, attachments, detached structures, fences, retaining walls, and other objects.

↓

Check plants, materials, and distances

Flag combustible mulch, trees, shrubs, juniper, arborvitae, Gambel oak, fences, retaining walls, firewood, and other materials when local rules make location or distance important.

↓

Show the issue in the model

Highlight the object, show the distance, name the source rule, and let the designer adjust the layout while preserving the design intent.

Research notes

Why this matters now

Vectorworks solar studies

Solar analysis depends on location, date, time, and north orientation; this is the design case for true georeferencing and 3D vegetation.

Colorado defensible-space zones

Colorado wildfire guidance organizes mitigation around 0-5 ft, 5-30 ft, and 30-100 ft structure ignition zones.

2025 Colorado Wildfire Resiliency Code

Public implementation summaries describe local adoption by April 1, 2026 and compliance/enforcement timing by July 1, 2026 for WUI jurisdictions.

Colorado turf and plant laws

SB24-005 and HB25-1113 make turf, artificial turf, invasive plants, and property type important spatial and jurisdictional checks.
