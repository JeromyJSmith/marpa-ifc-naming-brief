Project coordinates

Without georeferencing, the project loses its real-world position

A project origin, Cartesian coordinate system, and site georeference are not just survey details. They are what let design geometry line up with real space, other exports, maps, measurements, and future projects.

Without project coordinates

  • DWG/DXF, PDF, image, IFC, and worksheet evidence cannot be reliably aligned.
  • 2D hardscapes and plant symbols are harder to turn into accurate 3D objects.
  • Measurements can drift between sheets, design layers, and exported files.
  • Drone, LiDAR, GIS, survey, property, and site imagery cannot snap into the same model.
  • Quantity takeoff has weaker source confidence because geometry cannot prove where it sits.
  • Vectorworks writeback becomes less useful because enriched objects have no shared spatial anchor.

With project coordinates

  • Every export can share the same project origin, units, scale, rotation, and coordinate reference.
  • PDF, DWG/DXF, IFC, worksheet, image, and 3D evidence can be compared object-by-object.
  • Hardscapes, walls, planting beds, trees, and materials can be enriched into spatial BIM objects.
  • Cost analysis can group quantities by real location, phase, area, slope, exposure, or zone.
  • 3D viewers and dashboards can show cost, scope, and risk on the actual site model.
  • Enriched IFC can import back into Vectorworks with object identity and spatial context intact.
Downstream impact

Georeferencing turns exported drawings into a reusable project model

The goal is not to make Vectorworks more complicated. The goal is to make every downstream file line up so MARPA can automate takeoff, enrichment, pricing, review, and writeback.
